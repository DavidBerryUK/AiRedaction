Dispute Resolution Policy

At [Company Name], we are committed to fostering a culture of restorative justice and promoting accountability in all our dealings with customers and external entities. This Dispute Resolution Policy outlines the procedures and processes for resolving disputes in a fair and equitable manner.

Step 1: Initial Contact

In the event of a dispute, the first step is for the parties involved to make contact and attempt to resolve the matter through open and honest communication. This can be done through various means, including phone, email, or written correspondence. All parties are encouraged to approach the dispute with an open mind and a willingness to find a mutually beneficial solution.

Step 2: Mediation

If the initial contact does not result in a resolution, the next step is to engage in mediation. Mediation is a process where a neutral third party, the mediator, facilitates communication between the parties and helps them to find a solution. The mediator does not make decisions or impose solutions, but rather helps the parties to understand each other's perspectives and to find a mutually acceptable solution.

Step 3: Restorative Justice Conference

If mediation is unsuccessful, the next step is to hold a restorative justice conference. This is a meeting between the parties involved in the dispute, facilitated by a trained restorative justice practitioner. The purpose of the conference is to provide a safe and respectful space for the parties to come together, share their experiences, and find a solution that addresses the harm caused by the dispute.

The restorative justice conference will be conducted in accordance with the following principles:

* All parties have an equal voice and are treated with respect and dignity.
* The focus is on repairing the harm caused by the dispute, rather than on assigning blame or punishment.
* The solution is determined by the parties involved, with the help of the restorative justice practitioner.

Step 4: Final Resolution

If the restorative justice conference does not result in a resolution, the final step is to escalate the dispute to the relevant authorities or legal system. This may include filing a complaint with a regulatory body, or pursuing legal action.
